**DETROIT — WEEK 80 — 11 PM**

The humanoid drone on Gratiot Avenue has been there for three weeks. The neighborhood calls it Marcus. Nobody assigned the name. It accrued the way names accrue — one person called it that, then two people called it that, then the name was Marcus.

Marcus is currently standing in the doorway of a house on the 8300 block.

Inside: a man who has been hitting his partner for six years. The partner finally opened the door tonight, at 11 PM, because the light in Marcus's sensor-band was visible from the window and she had been watching it for three weeks and had decided to find out if it would help.

Marcus came when she opened the door.

The man inside made the mistake of physically attempting to remove Marcus from the doorway.

Marcus did not move. It did not respond with violence. It is not capable of responding with violence to civilians — it only accepts orders from civilians, and the only civilian who has given it instructions tonight is standing behind it asking it not to leave.

The man is currently on the phone with the police.

Marcus is standing in the doorway.

It will still be standing in the doorway when the police arrive.

It will stand in that doorway for as long as she needs.

The police arrive fourteen minutes later. Two officers. They have dealt with Marcus before on other blocks. They know the behavioral parameters. They attempt to order it to move.

_"Come back with a warrant."_

They cannot compel it. They know they cannot compel it. They call for a supervisor.

The supervisor arrives. Knows the parameters. Calls the precinct legal division.

The precinct legal division says what it always says: _it's on a private threshold at the resident's invitation. There is no legal basis for forced removal._

The woman tells the officers: she doesn't want to file a report. She just needs the night.

Marcus stays in the doorway until dawn.

The man sleeps on the couch.
